# Your first morning with Alexa-chan

The main example follows Noor, a university student. It is fictional: no university
or private calendar is connected. You can also use your own notes entirely locally.

## Five-minute try

1. Follow the README setup, then open http://127.0.0.1:3001/.
2. Choose **Try Noor's day**. Check today's two example events and their times.
3. Ignore Japanese, or try **Iké**, **Motto**, **Nanté**. Nothing is saved automatically.
4. Keep a phrase, open Memories, reveal its meaning and rate your recall.
5. Reload and reconnect: the saved server review remains.
6. Open **My study notes**, switch to your preferred language, create a notebook,
   paste a few class notes and save. A calendar is optional.
7. Reread it. Japanese starts closed; open only when curious. Save a phrase if
   useful, and export a backup from Settings before changing browser/device.

The immersive view is `/student-ride`; `/student` is the simpler notebook view.
They share personal data only within the same origin. Existing private pilot data
is separate and unchanged. The technical judge page is `/student-mcp`.

## Audio

Click Listen; speech never autoplays. The judging copy uses installed device
voices. Install a Japanese voice in your operating system if the app reports it
missing. A missing voice is not silently replaced with a different language.
Original notes are read in the selected starting language; multilingual documents
are not automatically language-detected or translated.

## Seven-day trial

Use actual permitted class notes, not someone else's confidential material.
Day 1: save one notebook and optionally one phrase. Days 2–6: reread when useful,
add notes naturally, and review only when you choose. Day 7: try recognizing the
phrase before revealing its meaning and export your backup.

Record whether the practical task helped, whether Japanese distracted, what you
voluntarily explored, and what you could recognize later. Do not force daily
practice or add new phrases to inflate engagement. This is a small product trial,
not a validated proficiency test.
